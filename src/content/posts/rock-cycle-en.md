No rock under your feet was born the way it is now. It may once have been magma, later ground into sand by wind and rain, pressed back into stone, and perhaps even melted all over again. Geologists call this process the rock cycle.

## Igneous rocks: it starts with magma

When magma cools slowly underground, minerals have plenty of time to grow large crystals, forming coarse-grained rocks like granite. When lava erupts at the surface and cools quickly, it forms fine-grained rocks like basalt. Most of the Main Range that runs down the Malay Peninsula is made of granite.

> Rocks do change. They just change far more slowly than a human lifetime.

## Sedimentary rocks: broken down and rebuilt

Weathering and erosion break rocks down into sand and mud, which rivers carry away and lay down layer by layer. The weight of the layers above compacts them, and minerals cement the grains together, producing sandstone, shale and limestone. Most fossils are preserved in sedimentary rocks.

## Metamorphic rocks: remade by heat and pressure

When rocks are buried deep underground, or sit close to magma, high temperature and pressure change their minerals and texture without melting them. Limestone becomes marble, and shale becomes slate.

Any of these rocks can eventually melt back into magma, and the cycle begins again.
